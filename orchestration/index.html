这是博士论文第 4 章的分项汇报：交通编排。第 3 章的结果表明，在路网约束区运力是一个区间，下沿由交通规则决定。我已经把可证明不死锁的安全放行做成并检验：它精确、从不死锁，但偏保守，所以现在最好的无死锁规则仍是整条路径预约。第 4 章要设计更宽而同样可靠的放行与编排，把下沿往上推；第一个规则对照正在运行。右上是朴素逐段申请的典型死锁：两辆载货车对向驶入单车道，各占一段、各等对方；右下是安全编排定理的思路：载货出发前，把通往下一个安全待命点的路段连同待命位一起预约好。

This briefing covers chapter 4 of the thesis: traffic orchestration. Chapter 3's results show that, where the network binds, capacity is an interval whose lower edge is set by the traffic rule. I have built and tested a provably deadlock-free safe release: it is exact and never deadlocks, but it is conservative, so the best deadlock-free rule is still whole-route reservation. Chapter 4 designs wider releases and orchestration that are just as reliable, to push that edge up; the first rule comparison is running. Top right is the typical deadlock of naive segment request: two loaded vehicles enter a single lane head-on, each holding one segment and waiting for the other's. Bottom right is the idea of the safe-orchestration theorem: before a loaded departure, book the road up to the next safe holding point together with a slot there.

Where chapter 4 sits: chapter 3 explains where capacity comes from and gives the interval; chapter 4 pushes its lower edge up; chapter 5 then spends money to raise the upper edge. The chapter answers five of this study's claims and hypotheses. The two-ceilings claim writes capacity as an interval; its lower edge is whole-route reservation today, and the rules chapter 4 delivers take over. The claim “from dispatching to traffic ordering” says that orchestration decides how much of the bound is achieved, through four levers: release order, waiting points, reservation mode and block granularity. The exchange-rate claim draws the boundary: when the plateau lies far below the bound, change the orchestration first, which is Okpo's main scenario; the erection mix sits near the bound with whole segments and returns to “orchestration first” once segmented. Why can segment request not be the lower edge? Because it deadlocks constantly. At the four-vehicle-subset task volumes, Okpo deadlocks about 26 times on a regular day and 83 at peak; at saturation, 1,911 times a day with 100 vehicles and 2,657 with 150. Each deadlock sends one vehicle off the network for 900 seconds, so at K = 100 at least about 30% of vehicle-hours are not on the network. I read physical rules instead: with no clearing the whole network soon gridlocks, faster with more vehicles; counted per move, about 40 to 50% of loaded erection moves are teleported at least once on the way to the dock.
